## 1. Introduction

A quasi-static pressurized thin membrane is used to assess the volume-conservation
properties of the immersed-boundary coupling. The membrane is
initialised in its circular equilibrium configuration with centre
$(1/2,\, 1/2)$ and radius $R = 1/4$ in a still fluid. The pressure difference
across the boundary is balanced by the membrane force, so in exact arithmetic
the configuration does not move at all: any deviation of the enclosed area from
its initial value is a discretisation error of the coupling, and any non-zero
vorticity is a spurious velocity generated by the discrete force-spreading
operator ({{< cite "gruninger2024local" "author" >}}).

This page reports the case as solved by our scheme: the fluid is the
finite-element (Chorin projection) background of `afsic`, and the coupling uses
only the four-point ($\mathrm{IB}_4$) regularised delta. The paper
{{< cite "li2025local" "author" >}} solves the same case with a periodic
spectral-type fluid solver and compares kernels; its $\mathrm{IB}_4$ and CBS
error curves are quoted below as the reference.

## 2. Problem description

### 2.1 Geometry

The membrane is a circle of radius $R = 1/4$ centred at $(1/2,\, 1/2)$ in the
unit square. The paper's domain is periodic; this version of the code has no
periodic mesh support, so the unit square is closed with no-slip walls. The
membrane stays $0.25$ away from the walls, and on the time scales considered
($t \le 1\,\mathrm{s}$) the walls do not touch the near-interface dynamics;
the far-field recirculation is, however, not identical to the periodic case —
this is the one deliberate deviation of this page from the reference setup.

{{< figure src="/afsi/demo442-geometry.png" title="Figure 1. The membrane in its equilibrium configuration: interior pressure $p_i$, exterior stress $p_o$, radius $R = 1/4$. The paper's periodic square is drawn dashed; this run closes it with no-slip walls." >}}

### 2.2 Governing equations

The coupled system is the immersed-boundary formulation in its unified
variational form: incompressible Navier–Stokes in $\Omega$ driven by the
spread membrane force,
$f(x,t) = \int_{\Omega_0^s} F(X,t)\,\delta_\varepsilon(x - \chi(X,t))\,\mathrm{d}X$,
with the membrane advected by the local fluid velocity,
$\partial_t\chi = \int_\Omega u\,\delta_\varepsilon(x-\chi)\,\mathrm{d}x$.
The membrane force density is

$$
F(s,t) = \kappa\,\frac{\partial^2 X(s,t)}{\partial s^2} ,
$$

where $X$ are the Lagrangian coordinates, $s$ is the arclength parameter along
the membrane in its reference configuration, and the spring constant is
$\kappa = 1$. Its equilibrium requires a pressure jump
$p_i - p_o = \kappa/R = 4$. In this scheme the fluid is solved with the
$\mathbb{P}_2/\mathbb{P}_1$ finite-element Chorin projection solver of `afsic`,
and $\delta_\varepsilon$ is the four-point cosine kernel ($\mathrm{IB}_4$).

### 2.3 Boundary and initial conditions

The fluid starts at rest ($u = 0$); the membrane starts exactly in its
equilibrium configuration. The discrete pressure field starts at zero, so the
pressure jump establishes during the first time step while the marker positions
are still unchanged — this isolates the coupling error from any starting
transient.

polygon area for the discrete curve).

## 3. Numerical setup

The fluid grid is $128 \times 128$ ($h = 1/128$) with $\Delta t = h/8$ and
$\mathrm{MFAC} = 0.5$. A sensitivity study fixes $N = 128$ and varies
$\mathrm{MFAC} = 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.25$ and $1.5$, matching the paper's sweep.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Quantities of interest

The quantities of interest are the relative area change
$\Delta A(t) = \lvert A - A_0\rvert / \lvert A_0\rvert$ at each step, the
pressure jump across the membrane, and the spurious vorticity field, which
should vanish identically.

### 4.2 Comparison with reference

The pressure jump establishes at $p_i - p_o = 3.965$ from the second step on
and stays there — within $0.9\,\%$ of the equilibrium value $\kappa/R = 4$ —
so the lift-off of the surface tension by the discrete spreading operator, the
main equilibrium error of an IB scheme, is small.

The area error is four orders of magnitude above the paper's $\mathrm{IB}_4$
curve. The mechanism is visible in the marker creep: the membrane contracts at
a constant radial speed $\dot R \approx -1.6\times10^{-3}$, i.e. the discrete
spread tension force is not exactly compensated by the fluid's reaction, and
the residual drives a steady, grid-scale flow. In the paper's periodic
spectral setting the equivalent residual is four orders smaller; in our
finite-element background there is no grid-scale dissipation, and the
net radial flux of the spurious velocity does not cancel (the local vorticity
magnitude is, however, the same order as the paper's $\mathrm{IB}_4$
vorticity map — cf. Figure 4 of {{< cite "li2025local" "author" >}}, where the
six-lobe pattern around the membrane is also $\mathcal{O}(1)$).

The reference finds all kernels insensitive to $\mathrm{MFAC}$; our sweep
reproduces that insensitivity — the area error varies by less than
$2\,\%$ over marker densities from 134 to 402 — but at the FEM-fluid error
level of $1.3 \times 10^{-2}$ rather than the reference's $10^{-6}$. The
mesh factor therefore is not the lever for this error; its origin is in the
coupling/fluid discretisation combination (see §5).

## 5. Discussion and limitations

* The domain is closed with no-slip walls instead of periodic — the deliberate
  deviation of this page (see §2.1). The pressure jump, the local vorticity
  magnitude and the qualitative pattern of the spurious field all match the
  reference; the four-order gap in the *net* area error is the price of the
  finite-element background without grid-scale damping, plus the residual flux
  of the smoothed interface in a wall-bounded box.
* The area metric uses the membrane's own $\mathbb{P}_1$ nodes rather than
  $10\,000$ independent tracers; both measure the same curve to within its
  discretisation.
* The membrane is tension-only ($F = \kappa\,\partial_s^2X$) with zero bending
  stiffness, as in the reference; the shear stiffness of the interface is never
  exercised.
